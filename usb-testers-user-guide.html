Grab this one for…

Just tell me if it's charging

DROK USB Meter

No protocols, no app, no drama. If the question is "is this cable actually delivering 2A," this answers it and gets out of the way.

Daily-carry sanity check

TC66C

Widest range of the pocket dongles (30V/5A), isolated-power precision mode, and PPS trigger — the one that earns a permanent spot in the bag.

Nostalgia / classic hobbyist pick

Still fine for logging capacity claims on sketchy power banks over Bluetooth. Just don't expect it to keep up with anything PD3.1.

Bad cable, or bad charger?

FNB58

Onboard scope plus E-marker and DASH cable reads mean you can point fingers at the actual culprit instead of guessing.

One step below lab-grade

50V/240W headroom, UFCS support, and USB4-rated pass-through — the most future-proof of the "just plug it in" testers.

The only one that actually decodes PD/UFCS packets instead of inferring them, and the only one with QC5 and PD3.1 called out by name.

Overall pick — if you enjoy voiding warranties

CT-3 (Shizuku)

Nothing else here runs your own code, swaps in a load module, or gives you four ways to measure the same cable's resistance out of spite.

Where all seven of these still fall short

  • USB4 / Thunderbolt signal compliance — the KM003C and FNB-C2 have USB4-rated connectors, which means continuity, not certification. None of these seven do actual eye-diagram or protocol-compliance testing on USB4 data lines; the CT-3's manual is the only one honest enough to say its data pass-through "isn't guaranteed."
  • PD 3.1 EPR at the top end — the KM003C is the only device here that explicitly lists PD3.1 and QC5 by name. If you're chasing a 240W laptop brick's full EPR negotiation, it's the only tool in this drawer built for it.
  • AC-side (wall) measurement — every device here measures the DC side of a USB port. None of them tell you wall-to-USB efficiency; that still needs a Kill-A-Watt or equivalent in the loop.
  • Traceable/calibrated accuracy — these are hobbyist-grade instruments (0.05%–1% class, uncalibrated). Fine for "is this charger lying," not fine for anything that needs a calibration certificate.
  • Newer proprietary fast-charge standards — things like the latest 300W+ laptop-specific protocols or brand-new EPR variants will outpace all seven eventually. When that happens, it's a firmware update away on the KM003C or FNB-C2, and a genuinely new purchase for the rest.
